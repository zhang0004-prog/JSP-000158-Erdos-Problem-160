## Abstract

This report documents recent progress on Erdős Problem #160, which asks for the asymptotic behavior of the minimum number of colors required to color \([N]=\{1,2,\ldots,N\}\) so that every nontrivial four-term arithmetic progression contains at least three distinct colors.

The work combines finite exhaustive computation, symmetry reduction, structural classification, arithmetic-progression constraint analysis, literature verification, and the development of a Fourier-analytic framework.

The project records several finite computational results, including the exact value \(h(22)=4\) and the lower bound \(h(N)\geq 5\) for all \(N\geq 23\), subject to the stated finite-search specifications and recorded verification.

This report does not claim a complete solution of Erdős Problem #160. The global asymptotic problem remains open. Several theoretical components remain conditional or under audit, and the distinction between verified finite results, conditional arguments, literature claims, and unresolved proof obligations is maintained throughout.

## 1. The Problem

Let \(h(N)\) denote the minimum number of colors required to color \([N]=\{1,\ldots,N\}\) such that every nontrivial four-term arithmetic progression

\[
a,\ a+d,\ a+2d,\ a+3d,\qquad d\ne 0,
\]

contains at least three distinct colors.

This research retains the original coloring condition. It does not identify the full problem with the narrower problem of avoiding symmetrically colored progressions.

## 2. Finite Computational Results

### 2.1 Three-color classification

The finite reproduction project records:

- At \(N=12\), exactly six valid three-colorings were found.
- At \(N=13\), no valid three-coloring exists.
- The corresponding numbers of nontrivial four-term progressions are 18 and 22.

Consequently,

\[
h(N)\geq 4\qquad(N\geq 13).
\]

The enumeration used Python-based exhaustive procedures and did not rely on SAT/DRAT certificates.

### 2.2 Four-color classification

The four-color computation records:

- At \(N=22\), exactly 144 valid four-colorings.
- These correspond to six canonical representatives under the 24 permutations of four colors.
- At \(N=23\), no valid four-coloring exists.

Combining the finite existence and nonexistence results gives

\[
\boxed{h(22)=4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{h(N)\geq 5\quad\text{for all }N\geq 23.}
\]

The computation also records agreement between prefix-normalized and full-search counts for \(N=1,\ldots,23\). These are finite results and are not presented as an asymptotic solution.

## 3. Symmetry and Structural Classification

The four-color investigation studies the action generated by:

- permutations of the four color labels;
- reversal of the interval, \(x\mapsto 23-x\).

The resulting symmetry group has order 48. Additional recorded results include:

- no valid full-period solutions with period at most seven;
- 4,272 valid colorings at \(N=21\);
- 178 canonical representatives at \(N=21\);
- 14 strictly seven-periodic representatives, none extending to \(N=22\);
- only five of the 178 canonical representatives extending to \(N=22\).

These findings provide a finite structural description of the solution space and its extension obstructions.

## 4. C4 Structural Investigation

The C4 project studies overlapping forbidden arithmetic-progression windows and possible local modifications of colorings. Recorded work includes:

- 45 violation windows in the baseline configuration;
- a maximum pairwise-disjoint family of five windows;
- an EXACT5 candidate space of 248,832 configurations;
- a classification project involving 36 AP10-XOR skeletons;
- exclusion of 12 zero-cover skeletons;
- correction and subsequent exclusion of SKEL_01.

The latest ledger retains 23 residual skeletons as **OPEN**.

The unconditional lower-bound status of this branch must not be overstated: a proposed strengthening to a bound of six has not been re-established in the latest corrected ledger. The currently retained unconditional bound is at least five.

## 5. Fourier-Analytic and Frequency-Decomposition Framework

The project has developed a frequency-based framework involving:

- global frequency partition reconciliation;
- kernel reconciliation;
- color-index alignment;
- strict positivity of the zero-frequency contribution;
- conditional implications concerning nonzero-frequency vanishing;
- translation-scope reconciliation for the E2 and E3a components.

The stage report records several internal components as **VERIFIED** or **PROVED**, while color-index alignment remains conditionally verified and the overall H0 argument remains conditional.

A separate universality audit is still required to determine whether the original H0 hypothesis covers every qualifying pair of distinct-step four-term arithmetic progressions with exactly two common points.

Accordingly, this framework is presented as an ongoing analytical development, not as a completed unconditional proof.

## 6. Literature Audits

The research examines the relationship between the original problem and related work on symmetric arithmetic-progression colorings. Relevant literature includes:

- Deng, Tidor, and Zhao, on colorings avoiding symmetrically colored arithmetic progressions;
- Shi and Dong, *An Improved Upper Bound for Colorings Without Symmetrically Colored k-Term Arithmetic Progressions*, arXiv:2607.20752;
- Raghavan, *Improved Bounds for 3-Progressions*, arXiv:2603.27045;
- work of Bloom and Sisask on three-term arithmetic progressions;
- Ruzsa’s 1993 work and its explicitly recorded proof omission.

The audits pay particular attention to hidden hypotheses, modular restrictions, lemma correspondence, and the validity of transferring results from symmetric-pattern avoidance to the original coloring problem. The full proof audit of the Raghavan route remains incomplete.

The work establishes a finite classification milestone, develops structural information about finite coloring spaces, and builds a developing analytical framework for investigating Erdős Problem #160.

\[
h(22)=4,\qquad h(N)\geq 5\quad(N\geq 23).

The broader problem remains open. The purpose of this report is to make the research record, finite results, methods, limitations, and outstanding proof obligations available for scrutiny and collaboration.
